## What the entry says

The entry opens with the name معاذ بن عمرو بن الجموح بن كعب الأنصاري and the labels
الخزرجي، السلمي، المدني، البدري، العقبي، then the title قاتل أبي جهل. Ibn Ishaq
supplies the full chain up to بن سلمة and says he witnessed Badr.

The body is the story of Abu Jahl's death at Badr. Two young Ansari men — Muadh
ibn Amr ibn al-Jumuh and Muadh ibn Afra' — asked Abd al-Rahman ibn Awf where Abu
Jahl was, then attacked him together. The Prophet ruled that both had killed him
and awarded the spoils to Muadh ibn Amr. Muadh's own account follows: he struck
Abu Jahl's foot and cut it at the shin; Abu Jahl's son Ikrimah then struck his
shoulder and his hand was torn off, hanging by a piece of skin. He fought the rest
of the day dragging it behind him, then put his foot on it and tore it away.
al-Dhahabi closes with the remark that this is courage of a kind no other wound
inflicts. Ibn Ishaq adds that he lived on into the time of Uthman.

The entry says nothing about Bir Ma'unah itself; the section heading groups the
entry there, and its content is Badr.

## Discrepancy left in the source

The entry dates his end twice, and the two do not agree: p1 of printed 250 says
روى عنه: ابن عباس، وعاش إلى أواخر خلافة عمر, while Ibn Ishaq's closing line on
printed 251 says ثم عاش بعد ذلك إلى زمن عثمان. Neither gives a year, so the model
holds no field for either and nothing was written. Both readings stay in the
account pages.
